Window Installation

Energy-efficient and impact-rated windows that stand up to Florida heat, humidity, and storms. Installed by the same crew that does your insulation.

A window is the weakest part of a Florida wall twice over. In August it is the piece that lets the sun straight into the room, and in September it is the piece a flying roof tile can go through. Replacing windows costs enough that it is worth knowing what you are buying before anyone hands you a quote.

What windows really cost you in a Central Florida summer

Insulation is rated on how well it slows conduction. Glass is a different problem: it is a solar aperture. On a west-facing wall in July, sunlight passes through the pane and becomes heat inside the room, and no amount of attic insulation stops it once it is there.

Every window carries an NFRC label with two figures worth reading. U-factor measures heat conducted through the assembly — the number that dominates in cold climates. Solar Heat Gain Coefficient, or SHGC, measures how much of the sun's energy the glass lets through on a scale from 0 to 1, and here it decides whether a room is usable at four in the afternoon. A low-SHGC Low-E coating reflects most of the infrared while passing visible light, so the room stays bright and stops behaving like a greenhouse.

Old windows also leak. Single-pane aluminum units have no thermal break, and after three decades of sun the seals and weatherstripping have usually given up. You get air exchange nobody asked for, and with it the humidity that makes a house feel warmer than the thermostat claims.

Impact glass, shutters, or neither

We install impact windows, and we still talk people out of them.

Impact windows use laminated glass: two panes bonded to a plastic interlayer, so the glass can crack and still stay in the opening. That matters less because of the hole than because of what a hole does to a house — once the envelope is breached in high wind, pressure inside rises against the roof deck from below. Impact units are permanent, and they cut noise and UV fading all year rather than three days a year.

They are also the most expensive option per opening. Accordion shutters and rated aluminum panels give code-level protection for a fraction of the cost; their weakness is human, in that someone has to be home and able to close them in time. If your windows are sound, under fifteen years old, and not fogging or sticking, shutters are frequently the better spend and we will say so. Impact glass makes obvious sense when the windows are failing anyway: fogged panes, rotted frames, sashes that will not lock. You are buying windows regardless.

What "impact rated" actually means

Impact rating is a tested certification tied to a specific product in a specific size, not a marketing word. Units carry a Florida Product Approval number or a Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance, showing they passed missile impact testing and thousands of pressure cycles. Each approval also carries a design pressure rating that must meet the wind load calculated for that opening, which differs between a second-storey gable end and a sheltered ground-floor wall on the same house.

Whether an address sits inside the wind-borne debris region comes from the local wind map rather than the county name, and it varies across Polk and Hillsborough. We check before specifying anything.

Frames and what the material changes

Vinyl is the default for most residential replacement: it insulates well, will not corrode in humidity, and is the value option in standard sizes. Aluminum is strongest per inch, buying slim sightlines and more glass, but untreated it conducts heat through the frame and wants a thermal break. Fiberglass and composite cost more and hold their shape better on large openings.

Full-frame or insert replacement

An insert, or pocket, replacement leaves the existing frame in the wall and fits the new unit inside it. It is cheaper and leaves stucco and trim alone, but you lose an inch or two of glass and keep whatever condition the old frame and flashing are in. Full-frame replacement goes back to the rough opening so flashing and waterproofing can be redone properly. It is the only honest option where there is rot or a history of leaks — an insert would simply hide it.

What the job actually involves

Every opening is measured individually, because older Florida homes settle out of square and windows ordered from one measurement across an elevation come back wrong. Impact units are built to order, so plan on weeks between deposit and install day.

Window replacement is permitted work in Florida, and the product approval paperwork goes in with the application; we file it and meet the inspector. Install day is usually one to three days for a whole house: old unit out, new unit set and squared, flashed and sealed, then caulk, trim and stucco patching. Leave the exterior alone until the final inspection passes.

What drives the price

There is no square-foot figure that means anything here, so we would rather explain the variables. Price is driven by the number and size of openings, whether the glass is impact-laminated or standard Low-E, the frame material, the design pressure required, insert versus full-frame, any rot or stucco repair that turns up, and access. Estimates are free and done in person.

Rebates and insurance

Windows are the upgrade where we usually have to tell people there is less money waiting than they hoped. Local utility programmes target attics and HVAC, not glass: Lakeland Electric offers $300 on an attic insulation upgrade where the existing insulation measures R-19 or less and a pre-inspection happens first, and Duke Energy, TECO and Florida Power & Light run separate programmes elsewhere. Federal incentives for windows have changed more than once lately, so confirm eligibility with your tax preparer before budgeting around it.

Insurance is the bigger lever. Florida carriers price wind risk from a wind mitigation inspection, on which opening protection is a credited item. Worth knowing before you spend: the credit generally requires every glazed opening protected to the same standard, sliders and skylights included. Ask your agent what your carrier credits before deciding how to phase the work.

Common questions

Will impact windows lower my insurance premium?

They can, through the opening protection credit on a wind mitigation inspection, though the amount varies by carrier. The credit normally depends on every glazed opening being protected, so a partial replacement often earns nothing until the last one is done.

Are impact windows worth it this far inland?

Inland Central Florida still sees hurricane-force gusts, and the debris in a built-up neighbourhood is mostly roof material from nearby houses. If your windows are in good condition, rated shutters give comparable storm protection for far less. The year-round arguments for impact glass inland are noise, UV and convenience.

How long does the whole process take?

Measuring and quoting is quick. Manufacturing is the long pole, since impact units are made to order — expect weeks, longer approaching storm season. Installation is typically one to three days, followed by a permit inspection.

Can I replace just a few windows at a time?

Yes, and phasing by room is a reasonable way to manage cost — worst west- and south-facing rooms first. Just note that comfort improves room by room while the insurance credit usually waits until every opening is protected.

Windows are one side of the envelope. Sealing the attic with spray foam insulation or topping it up with blown-in insulation usually returns more per dollar than glass does, and where material is contaminated or storm-damaged, insulation removal comes first.
