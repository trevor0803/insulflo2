How to Tell If You Need New Attic Insulation

Published 20 August 2026 · Insulflo Energy Services, Lakeland FL

Insulation is the only part of your house you never look at. It sits above the ceiling doing its job or failing at it, silently, for thirty years. Nobody notices it going — they notice the power bill, or the back bedroom that never cools, and they blame the air conditioner. Here is how to tell whether the attic is actually the problem, and you can check most of this yourself in ten minutes.

The thirty-second check that tells you most of what you need

Open the attic hatch, put a torch in, and look across the floor of the attic.

If you can see the tops of the wooden ceiling joists, you do not have enough insulation. That is the whole test. Those joists are usually somewhere between eight and ten inches deep. If the insulation is level with them or below them, you are sitting at roughly R-11 to R-19.

Most of Central Florida — Polk, Hillsborough, Pinellas — sits in climate zone 2, where the Florida Building Code sets a ceiling target of R-38 for new construction. Insulation should be burying that framing, not sitting between it like porridge in a tray.

You do not need to climb in. Head and shoulders through the hatch with a torch is enough.

The signs you feel before you see

1. One room never matches the rest of the house

A back bedroom, a bonus room over the garage, an upstairs that runs five degrees warmer than downstairs. When one part of a house consistently loses the fight, the ceiling above it is usually where the heat is coming in.

2. The air conditioner stops cycling off between about 3pm and 8pm

A healthy system runs, satisfies the thermostat, and rests. If yours runs continuously through the late afternoon and still does not quite get there, the house is taking on heat faster than the system can remove it. That is an envelope problem, not usually a mechanical one.

3. The ceiling is warm to the touch in the evening

This is the simplest test in the house and almost nobody does it. Around eight or nine at night, put your palm flat on the ceiling in the warmest room. It should feel the same as the wall. If it feels noticeably warmer, heat is coming through it — hours after the sun came off the roof.

4. The bill keeps climbing and nothing else changed

Same house, same thermostat setting, same number of people, and the August bill is higher than it was two summers ago. Rates move, so this is not proof on its own. But it is worth a look in the attic before you assume it is just the utility.

Signs it needs taking out, not topping up

These are different, and the difference matters because it changes the cost of the job considerably.

  • A smell you cannot place. If an odour in the house seems to come from above, look up. Rodent contamination soaks into loose fill and cannot be cleaned out of it.
  • Droppings, nesting, or tunnels through the insulation. Runs and hollows in an otherwise flat surface mean something has been living up there.
  • Staining on the insulation or the underside of the roof deck. Water has been in. Wet insulation mats down and never recovers its loft, and material that has been wet in a Florida attic frequently has mould in it.
  • It has been packed flat. Storage, foot traffic, or trades working in the attic crush it. Loft is most of what insulation does — flattened insulation is not insulating.

If none of those apply and the material is simply thin, leave it in. New insulation goes straight over the top and both layers count toward the total. Removing perfectly serviceable material costs a great deal more and buys you nothing.

Age is a decent shortcut

If your house was built in the seventies or eighties and nobody has been in the attic since, it is almost certainly well below current levels. Standards have moved a long way, and a lot of Plant City housing stock predates them. That is not a fault in the house — it is just what was normal at the time.

When it is not the insulation

We would rather tell you this now than after you have paid us.

If your ductwork runs through the attic — and in a great many homes here it does — then adding depth at the ceiling leaves that duct sitting in an attic that can run 30 to 50°F above the outdoor air. The cold air warms on the way to the room. In that situation, sealing at the roofline with spray foam does something that adding fluff at the ceiling cannot.

If nobody has ever sealed the gaps in your ceiling, insulation alone will disappoint you. A typical ceiling is punctured dozens of times — around the top plate of every interior wall, at wiring and plumbing penetrations, around bath fans and recessed lights. Insulation slows heat through a surface; it does nothing about air moving around it.

And sometimes it genuinely is the air conditioner — a failing capacitor, low refrigerant, a blocked return. If the system is old and struggling, get that looked at first.

What to do next

Do the joist check and the ceiling-touch test tonight. They cost nothing and between them they will tell you whether this is worth a phone call.

If it is, we come out and measure what is actually up there rather than guessing from the kerb. We will tell you honestly if your attic is in decent shape and does not need us — that happens more often than you would think, and it is a better business for us than the alternative.

Worth asking about while you are at it: Tampa Electric and Duke Energy both run residential efficiency programmes, and Lakeland Electric offers a rebate on attic insulation where the existing material measures R-19 or less. The amounts and the rules change, and some require an inspection before the work starts, so we check what is current for your address rather than quoting you a number from memory.

Depending on what we find, the answer is usually blown-in insulation at the ceiling, spray foam at the roofline, or insulation removal first if the old material has to come out.
